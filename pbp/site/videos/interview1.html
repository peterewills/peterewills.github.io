RUSA PBP Prep Interview Series #1

Cheryl Becker

Watch on YouTube ↗

Video: RUSA PBP Prep Interview Series #1 — Rob Hawks (host) interviews Cheryl Becker (RUSA member, San Francisco, rode PBP 2019 in the 84-hour group)

Series context (from intro)

  • This is a relaunch of an interview series last done roughly a year prior.
  • Upcoming: April 2 — a presentation on drop-bag/support services (presenter's name unclear in audio), plus Mark Thomas presenting on requirements/due dates for pre-registered PBP riders.
  • Cheryl's RUSA number is 7647 (Rob initially misstated it as 7649).

Background and path into randonneuring

  • Moved to San Francisco in 2007 and started cycling only after the move; not previously a cyclist.
  • Progression: metric century, then a century, then a double century the following year — got drawn into the Northern California double-century circuit.
  • Met people there (mentions Kenny Emerson and "Pudu") who introduced her to randonneuring; joined RUSA and rode her first brevet in 2012.
  • What appealed to her: pushing herself over longer distances without a competitive/racing atmosphere; found the randonneuring community welcoming from her first rides.

Qualifying rides and building confidence

  • 2014: rode the California Central Coast 1200 (CCC), a popular pre-PBP qualifier that year given the ACP pre-registration deadline pressure many riders were under.
  • After the CCC she developed Achilles issues and didn't ride again for about six months — an issue she says never recurred.
  • Years later rode the "Coulee Challenge" and had a strong, trouble-free experience — this ride convinced her the earlier Achilles problem had been a one-off (bike setup issue) rather than a sign she couldn't handle the distance.
  • PBP came onto her radar gradually — aware of it by 2015 through other randonneurs but didn't seriously consider it until 2019.

Preparing for PBP 2019

  • Describes herself as "not a planner" — training and preparation were minimal/informal, just continuing her normal riding.
  • Rode a 1000K in April 2019 (referred to as the "Gambler") specifically to prepare for PBP.
  • Completed the rest of her qualifying series (600K, 400K) later in the year, in the summer — after the 1000K, an unusual order that Rob notes reflects unusual confidence.
  • Rode the 84-hour start group; chose it because it was suggested to her and because friends she rides well with were also riding that group.

The PBP experience itself

  • Biggest surprise: the sheer scale of the event — hundreds of riders and volunteers at controls, well organized, compared to typical brevets where a control might have only ~20 riders.
  • Shared a common rando experience: struggling to find her bike after coming out of the cafeteria at a control (specifically at Loudéac).
  • Weather: rode into some rain the first night after Tinténiac heading toward Loudéac, but only got "a little bit damp" and wasn't badly affected — considers herself lucky compared to notoriously rainy PBP years.
  • Favorite control: Villaines-la-Juhel — the first town reached, ridden in daylight both outbound and inbound with the 84-hour group; remembers bikes lined up along the street and a strong crowd atmosphere.
  • Advice for first-timers: take time to actually enjoy the experience and soak in the atmosphere (crowds, cheering, ambiance) rather than just focusing on making time checkpoints.

Demographics and being cheered on as a woman

  • Rob notes the "typical" PBP rider is often characterized as roughly 49 and a half years old, male, non-racer — a profile Cheryl doesn't fit.
  • She says despite that stereotype, there was huge variety at PBP — many women, older and younger riders, and participants from all over the world — so she never felt out of place or underrepresented.
  • She agrees with Rob's observation that groups containing women get a noticeably bigger crowd reaction (more applause, more animated spectators, especially from women watching) in the small villages along the route.
  • No negative treatment or impediments at controls or from officials as a woman rider — reception was uniformly supportive and enthusiastic.

Quick-fire closing questions

  • Favorite control? Couldn't distinguish most controls by name ("they all sounded French") except Villaines-la-Juhel, which stood out.
  • Was the 84-hour group the right choice? Yes in retrospect — she has nothing to compare it to directly, but it worked and she finished.
  • Will she do PBP again? Uncertain — considered it this year but hasn't been riding enough to requalify; says "maybe next time." Notes that right after finishing in 2019 she initially thought "never again," but that feeling flipped to "I'll definitely do that again" not long after (an example of the randonneuring phenomenon "randonesia").
  • Closing message to prospective first-timers: it's an experience she's glad she had and one of the coolest things she's ever done — encourages others to go experience it themselves.
