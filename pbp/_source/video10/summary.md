**Video:** Randonneurs USA PBP Prep Interview Series #3 — Karen Nutter, interviewed by Rob Hawks (Karen has completed PBP twice, 2015 and 2019; rides with the Berkshire brevet series and New England Randonneurs).

**Background & Getting Into Randonneuring**
- Bought a bike in 2012 after giving up running; did charity rides (three-day, 100 miles/day) before wanting a bigger personal challenge.
- First randonneuring ride was a 300K in August, the year before PBP 2015.
- Discovered the Berkshire brevet series first, which led her to New England Randonneurs; learned about PBP only after starting the series and realizing a full brevet series (200/300/400/600K) qualifies a rider for PBP.
- Initially thought PBP sounded "crazy" but was drawn to it as a challenge after reading blogs and accounts.

**First 600K / Early Randonneuring Lessons**
- Rode her qualifying 600K in Virginia/DC/Carolina area after knee meniscus surgery over the winter, so avoided hilly terrain near home.
- Rode much of it solo but found riding partners at night to stay motivated; endured a severe thunderstorm (group sheltered under a church roof) and long stretches between controls with no stores (had to knock on a stranger's door for water/ice).
- Take-away: randonneuring means "you get by however you can and rely on other randonneurs."

**Training Approach: 2015 vs. 2019**
- Hired a trainer for both 2015 and 2019 — called it "an extraordinary help" and "the best choice" both times, since it removed the burden of planning ride volume/type.
- 2015: brevet series was a steep learning curve, building from 200K to 300K to 400K to 600K.
- 2019: rode a lot of brevets in the year before (2018) and again in 2019 leading up to PBP, and "felt so ready" by the time she arrived — a completely different mental state than 2015's nervous/anxious "give it a shot" mindset.
- Kept detailed, evolving packing lists starting from her first 200K, refined through each successive distance; grew to two pages, organized by what goes in front bag, handlebar bag, and rear bag — by PBP she didn't have to think about gear, and could just follow her trainer's daily workout instructions.
- 2015 gear mishap: a rear bag secured with a bungee cord at the last minute came loose about a mile down the road; she had to walk her bike back a mile to get help from mechanical/technical support.

**Start Group Choice: 90-Hour vs. 84-Hour**
- 2015: started in the 90-hour group, finished in 88 hours.
- 2019: switched to the 84-hour (daytime) start, finished in 80 hours.
- Found the night start (90-hour group) much harder — pre-ride nerves/anxiety made pre-start napping impossible, and exhaustion hit earlier than expected.
- 2019's morning start let her sleep at night instead of riding through it (roughly midnight–4/5am), which she believes also helped her avoid the notorious overnight cold/chill.
- Says she felt stronger the whole ride in 2019 and would pick the 84-hour start again "absolutely" if she rode PBP a third time; doesn't think she could have replicated her 80-hour finish starting with the 90-hour group.

**Sleep Strategy**
- 2015: minimal sleep, a couple hours at a time; slept twice on the roadside (once in drizzle), sometimes joining other sleeping randonneurs under a shared blanket.
- 2019: much more organized — stopped roughly three times outside her main sleep breaks (once warming up with a blanket and hot coffee at a rest stop, twice napping roadside on cobblestones for about 30 minutes when needed).
- Estimates 2015 sleep total was low (roughly 2 hours x 3 times); 2019 she got at least 4–5 hours of sleep across three sleep stops — "made all the difference."
- Learned from 2015: if you start wobbling or drifting from fatigue, get off the bike and nap rather than pushing through — she witnessed people wobbling and a couple of serious crashes from falling asleep on the bike.
- Her husband followed by car both years, meeting her at rest stops (not required to be at overnight sleep stops) with food from grocery stores when French control food didn't sit well; she recommends bringing Pepto-Bismol and Imodium. She slept in the back seat of the car during planned sleep breaks, setting an alarm to get back on the road.

**Confidence, Gear & Other Reflections**
- Describes herself as a stronger, more confident rider in 2019 versus 2015, attributing it to experience gained riding brevets and learning to problem-solve (e.g., she attempted (but did not finish) a 1000K in Nova Scotia due to extremely strong winds and made the call to stop at night alone — called it a learning experience, not a failure).
- On advice: heard many, sometimes contradictory, PBP stories from fellow brevet riders beforehand; learned "you can't listen to everything... that might not be the right advice for you."
- Biggest vital advice for first-timers: time management — accounting for long control lines (bathroom, food, and sign-in sometimes in different locations) and packing clothing properly for weather (hit rain on the last day in 2015).
- On being a woman on PBP: didn't feel treated differently from men; near the finish in 2019 two Swedish riders and a growing group (eventually ~10 riders) pulled her along to the finish line — attributes this to camaraderie, not gender.
- 2019 Achilles tendon issue: had to stop at the control in Villaines (return leg), got medical help from a nurse who taped her up (had to send out for tape since none was on hand) and gave her a leg massage; cost about an hour but she had enough time banked and it turned her ride around. Praised the PBP volunteer medical staff and village/town support along the route (village residents cheering riders on even overnight, small impromptu open coffee stops in the middle of the night).
- 2019 upgraded to a generator hub (vs. relying on batteries/chargers in 2015), which she said removed significant anxiety about light failure and was a major stress-reliever with no perceived speed cost.
- Rode significant portions of the 2019 course with fellow randonneurs Ed and Mary (from DC), picking up their wheel around Villaines and riding together to Tinténiac and Loudéac and much of days two and three.

**Rapid-Fire / Closing Questions**
- Would you pick the 84-hour start again? "84, absolutely."
- Could you have done an 80-hour finish from the 90-hour group? Not confident she could have, starting at night.
- Anything not asked? Highlighted the generator hub upgrade as the standout 2019 change (see above).
- Another PBP in her future? Uncertain — "maybe in four years... we'll see," possibly a US event instead.
